Nothing found beats the Lian Li O11 Dynamic EVO XL for this specific build. The two genuine alternatives are both real and both cheaper, but each trades away something that matters more than the money.

🏆
#1 Lian Li O11 Dynamic EVO XL — 531.9 mm. Biggest air plenum under the flow-through PRO 6000 — now measured at 99–120 mm against 62.8 mm in the C8 — the fat 65 mm Astral radiator drops in the roof the conventional way, 304 mm wide, 169 mm of published GPU height clearance, and the broadest stock in India.
🥈
#2 Antec C8 — 476 mm, ₹9,599. 56 mm shorter and ₹14k cheaper. But verification cut its headline advantage in half (see below), it publishes no GPU height clearance at all, the Astral rad has to go in an ambiguously-specced side bay, and only two shops in India have it.
🥉

Bonus: you can drop the desk lock entirely. All three are under 560 mm, so all three are shorter than the desk's lowest underside (~605 mm). The desk physically cannot reach them even if someone drives it to the floor. 01 The one number that changed the ranking

The first research pass ranked the Antec C8 first. An adversarial verifier then measured Antec's own orthographic drawings out of the official manual — calibrated on the PCIe bracket pitch, which self-checks to 20.35 mm against the 20.32 mm ATX spec — and refuted the number the whole recommendation rested on.

52 mm
C8's actual air plenum under the PRO 6000
110 mm
what the first pass claimed
73 mm
C8 absolute best case (card 3 pitches down)

Measured from the case's internal floor: only 62.8 mm to the bottom edge of the motherboard, and 47.0 mm to the centre of the lowest expansion slot. Put a 25 mm floor fan in and the breathing space under the card is 52.5 mm at four slot-pitches down — less than half what was claimed. The C8 still physically fits everything; it just doesn't feed the card as well as advertised.

🫁
This matters because the PRO 6000 is double-flow-through — it inhales from underneath. The plenum under it is the cooling system. The EVO XL's bottom chamber is far deeper, and its motherboard tray is height-adjustable, so you can raise the board and buy yourself even more. That is why it went back to first.

02 The three options, in order

Recommended · hold what you have

1 · Lian Li O11 Dynamic EVO XL

531.9 mm tall · 304 mm wide · 79.9 L · 8 slots · ₹24,998 (ARGB black, verified in stock at EliteHubs, 25 Jul 2026)

  • ＋ Deepest plenum under the GPU of the three, plus a height-adjustable motherboard tray so you can raise the board and open it up further.
  • ＋ The 65 mm Astral radiator goes in the roof — the simple, conventional placement, with clearance to spare.
  • ＋ 3 × 140 mm filtered floor intake, GamersNexus-verified as effective for bottom-ingesting cards.
  • ＋ Already fully documented — the cutaway diagram and the plain-English assembler sheet both describe this exact case. Zero re-work.
  • ＋ Widest retail availability of any candidate.
  • － Ships with zero fans, and its included GB-003 anti-sag bracket only works in the 2nd expansion slot — useless for the PRO 6000 lower down.
  • － Most expensive, and the tallest of the three.
Cheapest & shortest

2 · Antec C8 (plain, non-ARGB, black)

476 mm tall · 303 mm wide · 66.9 L · 8 slots · ₹9,599 (verified in stock at antecindia.in, 25 Jul 2026) · ~₹14,067 all-in

  • ＋ 56 mm shorter than the EVO XL and ₹15k cheaper. 78 mm of clearance even on a 2″ pedestal.
  • ＋ Top and right panels are full mesh from the factory — no separate mesh kit to hunt for, which is exactly the EVO XL's open problem.
  • ＋ Three simultaneous 360 mm mounts, full floor cut-out with no shroud in the way, pull-out floor filter you can clean without lifting the machine.
  • ＋ 303 mm wide — as roomy across as the EVO XL where it counts.
  • － Plenum is 52 mm, not 110 mm. The reason it lost first place.
  • － Roof envelope is 70 mm vs the Astral rad's 65 mm — only 5 mm — so the fat rad is pushed to the side bay, where Antec publishes two contradictory numbers (a 100 mm thickness envelope and a 50 mm side-radiator depth clearance, with no datum given for either).
  • － PSU bay is 210 mm excluding cables; the AX1600i is 200 mm. 10 mm is not enough for the connector face and bend radius — needs a short/90° cable kit.
  • － Two sellers in India, and three more have already sold out. That is the exact failure mode that killed the V3000 Plus.
  • － Buy the plain C8, not the ARGB — the ARGB's 2 × 160 mm bottom fans can't be repositioned or used with the floor mount.
Best airflow mechanism · worst fit for your taste

3 · Antec Performance 1 FT

522 mm tall · 230 mm wide · 62.7 L · 8 slots · ₹13,595–16,550 · out of stock at EliteHubs

  • ＋ The only survivor that reproduces the V3000's two-stage feed: 3 × 120 mm fans mounted on a perforated PSU shroud, drawing through the bottom filter and blowing straight up into the underside of the graphics card. This is the mechanism the whole case choice is about.
  • ＋ Roof question resolved: the roof mount is offset sideways away from the motherboard (vent 128 mm wide, sitting 74 mm clear of the tray side), so the 65 mm Astral radiator fits by hanging beside the board, not over it. It was excluded from the first draft on a false "unmeasurable" premise.
  • ＋ PSU bay 245 mm — the AX1600i drops in with 45 mm to spare, the cleanest power fit of the three.
  • ＋ Front bay takes the 52 mm LT720 comfortably (Antec publishes a 60 mm front cap).
  • － 230 mm wide. You rejected the 245 mm Flux Pro for feeling small and tight. This is 15 mm narrower than that.
  • － Roof clearance is measured, not published — ~28 mm lateral margin from the radiator's inner edge to the board plane. Tall RAM heatspreaders or a tall top-edge VRM heatsink on the X870E Carbon could touch. Use low-profile RAM.
  • － KitGuru measured the shroud fans making no temperature difference — though that was with a conventional card, not a bottom-ingesting one, so it may not transfer.
  • － Shroud fans not included; no anti-sag bracket in the box.

05 Before you buy — the open items

If you keep the EVO XL (recommended)

✅
The mesh side panel item on this list was a false premise and is now closed. No such Lian Li part exists, and the case does not need one — the top and right panels are factory mesh. The real pre-purchase gate is now four different conditions: open the pre-purchase checklist →

If you switch to the C8

  • Settle the side-bay conflict. Antec publishes a 100 mm thickness envelope and a 50 mm side-radiator depth clearance. The 65 mm Astral rad depends on which one is real — ask Antec India in writing before ordering.
  • Measure the gap from the floor-fan tops to the underside of the lower card on arrival. Antec publishes nothing here; 52 mm is derived from their drawings.
  • Plan the AX1600i cable exit — 200 mm PSU in a 210 mm bay measured without cables. Budget a 90° or short-cable kit.
  • Order the plain C8, not the ARGB, and treat the bare floor dust filter as fragile — it is the PRO 6000's only air path. Source a spare early.

If you switch to the Performance 1 FT

  • Accept the 230 mm width with your eyes open — it is narrower than the case you already rejected as too tight.
  • Use low-profile RAM. Roof clearance beside the board is ~28 mm, measured rather than published.
  • Budget 3 × 120 mm shroud fans separately; they are not included.
  • Find live stock first — it was out at every shop readable from here.

Applies to all three

  • Slot order is unchanged: PRO 6000 in the lower slot (over the floor fans, PCIe 5.0 ×4), Astral 5090 in the top slot (×8). Never flip these — the lower card is the one that breathes from below.
  • Never put a radiator on the floor. The floor is the PRO 6000's air supply.
  • The ROG Astral LC itself is scarce in India — check live stock before committing the rest of the build.
  • Instrument VRM and PCIe retimer temperatures, not just GPU temperatures.

06 What was ruled out, and why

63 unique chassis were swept across five parallel angles — Indian retail, the full Lian Li and Corsair line-ups, Antec/Cooler Master/Thermaltake/DeepCool/NZXT/Montech, the boutique airflow brands, and real multi-GPU AI build logs — then a completeness critic went hunting for what the sweep missed.
