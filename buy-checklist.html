Which case to buy

Nothing beat the Lian Li. Full detail lives on the build plan.

Your slot question: YES.
The 5090 goes on top, the PRO 6000 below it, and it works in all three tray positions — Lian Li's CAD file shows the 8 expansion slots are part of the motherboard tray assembly, so they move with it. Tray height can never change which case slot a board slot lands on.
Board slotCase slots used
Astral 5090 LC (2.5-slot)PCI_E12 + 3
RTX PRO 6000 (2-slot)PCI_E26 + 7
PCI_E1 → PCI_E2 measures 81.0 mm = exactly 4 slot pitches. 33.3 mm of clear air between the cards, slot 8 stays free, and both brackets land on real screwable positions — nothing hangs below the last slot. Still use the MIDDLE tray position, but for cooling, not fitment: it gives 82 mm roof (clears your 65 mm radiator) and 90 mm floor (feeds the PRO 6000).

Practical bit — seat the PRO 6000 FIRST. MSI's quick-release button only serves PCI_E1; PCI_E2 has a conventional latch you'd have to reach under the 5090. To ever remove the PRO 6000 you must pull the 5090 first.
A correction to what I told you earlier. I said "30.5 mm between the cards, so a straight cable won't fit." Wrong reasoning — a card's top edge faces the side panel, not the card above it. The number that actually governs is 169 mm case clearance − 137 mm card = 32 mm to the panel. Same conclusion (an angled cable is mandatory), arrived at properly. And it re-flags the real tightest item: the Astral has only 15.3 mm for its connector and coolant tubes.

How to get full output

  1. Tray in the MIDDLE. 82 mm roof / 90 mm floor. Low starves the GPU, Top won't take the radiator.
  2. Astral radiator in the ROOF, blowing out. Route both EPS cables first — no hand access after.
  3. CPU radiator on the SIDE, blowing in. Fit the two supplied seal plates.
  4. Three 140 mm fans on the floor, blowing in. Never a radiator there. Push them fully back and inboard.
  5. One 120 mm at the rear, blowing out — there's no 140 mm rear mount.
  6. Seat the PRO 6000 before the 5090, lower slot. Never swap the cards over.
  7. Fan daemon before the first real workload. Worth 15–19 °C — more than the case choice.

Downsides

  • The glass can shatter — real, documented, and your rolled-pedestal plan hits three of the four known triggers. Handle it by the steel.
  • The floor filter is the only fresh-air path to the GPU. Your carpenter's pedestal fixes the servicing side — spec at the bottom.
  • Ships with zero fans; side intake is unfiltered; poor after-sales (you've said you don't care).

Your existing fans — partial reuse

The TAG Gamerz Supernova has 7 fans and every one is 120 mm — no 140s. 3 side, 3 floor, 1 rear, ARGB+PWM on a hub.
  • Don't use them for the floor bank. TAG's own marketing calls them "low-impedance" fans — that's the industry phrase for low static pressure. Your floor bank is the opposite job: pull through a fine filter, push through 92.9 mm of plenum, into the dense fin stack of a 600 W card that's also pulling. TAG publishes no CFM, no static pressure, no RPM, no bearing type — nothing, anywhere. Harvesting also forfeits the 3×140 option, which gives 420 mm of coverage under a 305 mm card instead of 360 mm, at lower RPM — which matters a lot running 24×7 under a desk.
  • Do reuse exactly one as the rear exhaust. Low-demand job, the rear mount is 120 mm anyway, and failure there is harmless. Saves one of your four fan purchases.
  • Keep the other six as free spares. Zero cost, zero risk — just don't design around them.
30-second check first: pull one out and look at the cable end. A 4-pin PWM plus a separate 3-pin ARGB plug means it goes straight into the motherboard. One wide keyed plug means it's hub-locked — not worth dragging a controller into a 24×7 headless box for one fan, just buy it.

Powering the bare PRO 6000

Buy: Corsair Type-4 90° 12V-2x6 cable, SKU CP-8920348 — Style B. 650 mm, 16 AWG, 2× Type-4 8-pin → one moulded 12V-2x6. Style B exits DOWN along the card's face; Style A loops UP over the top and needs headroom you don't have. (Confirmed: the AX1600i is a Type-4 platform — Corsair's compatibility table has an "AXi / 1600 Only" column for exactly this reason.)

Avoid: any angled 12V-2x6 adapter — the 2024 CPSC recall covered CableMod's angled adapters (25,300 units, 272 melting reports), not angled cables. An adapter adds a second mated pair and hangs weight off the connector. That rules out Corsair's own ₹5,512 "GPU Power Bridge" too. Also avoid the Type 5 version — Amazon.in sells it at nearly the same price and it will not work on your PSU. The plugs are stamped Type 4 / Type 5; read the stamp.

Wiring: 600 W = 50 A. Corsair sanctions 2× 8-pin at 25 A per socket, but both must go to separate PSU sockets — never a pigtail. NVIDIA's own quick-start guide for this card specifies four separate 8-pin runs into a quad adapter, which halves the current per socket — better for 24×7. That quad adapter is one of the things missing from your bare import; worth sourcing as a spare.

Check per-socket OCP in iCUE before the first 600 W run — the AX1600i has configurable over-current protection on each socket and 25 A can nuisance-trip a default setting.
On first boot, run nvidia-smi. If it reports 84 GB, your imported card is the China-market RTX 6000D — cut-down cores, no NVLink — not the 96 GB global RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell. Same connector and same 600 W either way, but it's a different product and worth knowing which you have.

Pedestal — for your carpenter

  • Open frame only. Rails under the four feet, nothing crossing the middle. A solid deck seals the floor intake and suffocates the GPU. This rule cannot be broken.
  • Clear opening at least 480 × 280 mm so the whole filter area breathes.
  • Leave the filter's slide-out path completely clear — no rail, no lip, no caster in the way. This is the big win: you clean the filter without lifting a 40 kg machine.
  • Total height 25–50 mm including castors, rated 60 kg+, four locking castors, steel or hardwood.
  • Build to a 537 × 304 mm footprint — the 537 includes the rear brackets that stick out past the published depth.
