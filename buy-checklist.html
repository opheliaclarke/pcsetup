01 What I told you that was wrong

Four things I previously stated turned out to be incorrect. They are corrected everywhere on the site now, but you should know what changed and why.

I saidActuallyHow it was settled
"The mesh side panel is an unresolved blocker — every Lian Li mesh kit is out of stock." No such part exists, and none is needed. The side radiator breathes through the factory mesh right panel. Lian Li's own side-bracket figures (81 mm main chamber / 40 mm second chamber) only reconcile if the fans blow across the case width. Two agents independently parsed Lian Li's 78 MB CAD model and both landed on the same geometry to ~1 mm. Lian Li also ships 2 side dust filters, and GamersNexus benchmarked this case in a side-intake configuration.
"Roof takes an 81 mm stack." 82 mm — and 81 mm was the side bracket figure, misattributed. Roof and floor share a fixed 172 mm budget split by tray position. Lian Li manual p.28: Middle 82/90, Low 118/54, Top 46/126. The conflicting "118 mm" I'd seen quoted is real but is a different, mutually exclusive tray mode.
"1× 140 mm rear exhaust fan." There is no 140 mm rear mount. It's 120 mm. All six lenses agreed independently. Lian Li spec: "Rear: up to 2 × 120mm". The second rear fan requires Low tray mode, which costs 36 mm of floor plenum — so one 120 mm.
"Crush risk: eliminated by geometry." Conditional. 605 mm is the tabletop underside — the control box and crossbar hang below it. A LOGICDATA COMPACT control box is ~37 mm deep. That turns 48 mm of clearance into 11 mm on a 1″ pedestal, and into a collision on a 2″ one. See condition C4.

One thing the devil's-advocate lens landed that I'm passing on straight: "already documented for the assembler" was worth zero and should never have been in the comparison. layout.html and simple.html were drawn for the V3000 Plus, a different case with a fan shelf this one doesn't have — and they carry the 140 mm rear-fan error. Do not hand them to a builder. They need a from-scratch redraw.

02 The four conditions — settle these before money moves

C1 · 5 minutes · do this first

Order by SKU, never by name

Get the retailer to confirm in writing that the item is model O11DEXL-X, retail code G99.O11DEXL-X.IN. Lian Li makes exactly one EVO XL in two colours — there is no separate "EVO XL ARGB" product; the base case already carries the front ARGB strip. Every Indian listing titled "ARGB" prints the standard SKU underneath.

If they won't confirm: the dangerous near-miss is the O11 Dynamic EVO RGB (O11DERGBX) — a genuinely different mid-tower, 478 × 290 × 471 mm, 7 slots, 1 dust filter, sold at the same shops for ₹17,990. Buy from MD Computers instead; their product page prints MODEL O11DEXL-X.
C2 · you already own both cards

Measure the two cards' real installed height

Published GPU height clearance is 169 mm. The PRO 6000 at 137 mm leaves 32 mm; the Astral 5090 at 153.7 mm leaves 15.3 mm. Both cards carry 12V-2×6 on the top edge, which in this case faces the side glass. Lay each card flat, seat the power cable, and measure from the PCB card-edge datum to the highest point of the seated connector plus its first bend. For the Astral, also check where the two coolant tubes exit — ASUS publishes nothing on this.

If it comes back over 169 mm: this is not an EVO XL problem. The Antec C8 is 1 mm narrower and doesn't publish the figure at all. A bad answer means you need a wider case class entirely — which is a different research job, and far better learnt now than after the box is open.
C3 · one phone call

Confirm the India warranty path

Warranty is 1 year, void on serial-label damage. The entire published India after-sales channel is one line on Lian Li's warranty page: "India : Abhijeet Singh 7042202227." No address, no email, no portal. Ask whether they honour online-retailer invoices.

If the answer is bad or nobody picks up: pay the ₹1,800 premium and buy from MD Computers (₹25,000) — the most established of the five, sells the .IN-channel SKU and prints "Warranty 1 Year" on the listing. On a chassis wrapped around ~₹15 lakh of GPUs, ₹1,800 for a working RMA path is not a decision.
C4 · the one that actually worries me

Measure the desk's real lowest hard point — with the bay empty

I previously told you the crush problem was eliminated by geometry. That was based on the desk's tabletop underside at ~605 mm. But the control box hangs below the tabletop (a LOGICDATA COMPACT is ~37 mm deep with three motor sockets), the frame crossbar depth is unpublished, and "1 inch pedestal" describes the frame, not the frame plus casters — furniture casters run 40–70 mm plate-to-floor and can eat the entire 73 mm budget on their own.

How to settle it: with the bay empty, drive the desk fully down and measure floor → the lowest hard point anywhere over the PC's footprint: tabletop underside, crossbar, control box, cable tray. That one number replaces every calculation on this site.

If it comes back tight: the control box relocates toward a leg (four screws), and you can still program a LOGICDATA container stop above the mechanical minimum — the procedure is already in the project notes. Do not buy a 2-inch pedestal. A 1-inch pedestal is fine only if the total assembled lift is genuinely ≤25–30 mm; otherwise use a hard flat riser with no wheels, or stand it on the floor.

Three corrections to the layout you already had

  • Rear fan is 120 mm, not 140. There is no 140 mm rear mount on this case.
  • Push the floor fan block rearward. A 305 mm card runs from Z ≈ +245 back to Z ≈ −60; three 140 mm fans on the 479 mm bracket pushed fully rearward span Z −176 → +244. Centred or forward, roughly one of the three blows past the nose of the card into empty space.
  • Route both EPS cables before fitting the roof radiator. The 17 mm of roof margin sits above the motherboard's top edge, exactly where the X870E Carbon's two 8-pin EPS12V connectors live — and Lian Li never states the datum for its 82 mm. Right-angle EPS adapters (~₹300) settle it definitively.

Why the radiators are not swapped

Only one radiator here can breathe outside air — the floor is reserved for the PRO 6000 and the front takes nothing. So the question is which loop gets it. Making the Astral rad the intake would dump 600 W into the chamber the PRO 6000 breathes, taking case load from 600 W to 1,200 W. Disqualified. So the Astral must exhaust, and only the roof is big enough — which leaves the LT720 on the side breathing room air. A ~200 W CPU on a 360 fed with 30 °C air is a solved problem. Under the swap neither radiator gets fresh air, both breathe case air preheated to ~42 °C, and the CPU lands ~10 °C worse for 13 mm of screwdriver room.

Heat balance: with the CPU rad as side intake the chamber carries ~800 W, needing ~145 CFM to hold +10 °C. Floor 3 × 140 plus side 3 × 120 realistically deliver 250–300 CFM. The case does not heat-saturate — this layout is not marginal.

05 The day the box arrives

⏱️
Return windows in India run 7–10 days, and several of these checks need your own hardware dry-fitted. Do items 1–4 within 48 hours, before you build anything — if one fails you want the box still returnable.
  1. Before opening, read the carton label. It must say O11DEXL-X or G99.O11DEXL-X.IN. Then count 8 expansion slots and 4 dust filters (1 bottom tray, 1 top magnetic, 2 side magnetic). If it's 7 slots and one filter it's the mid-tower EVO RGB — refuse the delivery.
  2. Dry-fit the Astral 5090 in the top slot. Measure card top edge → side-glass mounting plane; expect 15.3 mm. Check the 12V-2×6 plug and both coolant tubes clear it. If they don't, the side panel won't close, and no ~304 mm dual-chamber case fixes that — stop and re-scope while the case is still returnable.
  3. Dry-fit the PRO 6000 in the lower slot with your chosen 90° adapter; expect 32 mm. A straight 12V-2×6 needs ~35 mm of run before any bend and you have 32.
  4. Confirm the side bracket breathes through the right mesh panel, not the front glass. Visual first, then a tissue test with a fan spinning. If it faces the front glass: pull the front glass — it's clip-mounted, the screws are transport-only — and fit a ~500 × 240 mm magnetic filter for ₹500–1,500. Build proceeds unchanged. Do not chase the Front Mesh Kit.
  5. Set the tray to Middle and measure roof clearance from the top bracket's underside to the installed motherboard; expect 82 mm. Route both EPS cables, then dry-fit the 400 mm rad. If it fouls, use right-angle EPS adapters — do not move the tray.
  6. Dry-fit the LT720 on the side bracket and check the tubes actually reach the CPU block around the front edge of the tray. Straight-line ~285 mm; realistic routed length 350–450 mm. If they don't reach: swap — LT720 to the roof, Astral to the side, both as exhaust. Accept ~10 °C worse CPU. This is the defined fallback.
  7. Measure floor-fan top face → PRO 6000 underside. Expect 99–120 mm. Below 80 mm, re-check the tray position. Record this number — it is the flow-through card's entire air supply.
  8. Confirm the rear opening is 120 mm (holes 105 mm square, not 124.5) before the fan order ships.
  9. Fit the two supplied seal plates in the side bay to blank the unused 420-length gap.
  10. Measure floor → top of case in situ, loaded, on whatever it stands on — then floor → the desk's lowest hard point. Under ~50 mm, drop the pedestal or program a desk stop.
  11. Set the case ≥100 mm off the wall, measured from the PCI bracket plane. The case is 537 mm deep in reality, not the published 522 — the brackets reach ~15 mm past the published rear plane.
  12. Photograph the GST invoice and the serial label together and keep both. Lian Li India refuses claims without valid proof of purchase, and a damaged serial label voids it outright.
  13. Before the first sustained workload, install the fan daemon. Proven curve from the dual-PRO-6000 report: ≤40 °C → 30%, 50 °C → 55%, 55 °C → 75%, 60 °C → 90%, ≥65 °C → 100%. Log GPU core and memory and board sensors at ≥1 Hz via NVML. Alarm on the pre-crash signature: core >85 °C while fan <70%.
  14. Re-check GPU temps at 60 days to quantify filter loading. GamersNexus measured 3 °C on a GPU from a bottom dust filter alone, on a clean case. Budget a monthly filter clean.

07 The alternative, stated fairly

The devil's-advocate lens attacked the EVO XL on four fronts and landed one. Here is the honest case for the Antec C8: it saves ₹13,600, it is 56 mm shorter (which more than doubles worst-case under-desk headroom from 48 mm to 104 mm), and it physically fits everything.

Why it still loses: floor plenum 90 mm published vs 62.8 mm measured — a ~43% deficit on the one dimension that feeds the card which must be fed from below. Antec does not publish GPU height clearance at all — the spec field doesn't exist — so you'd be gambling a 137 mm extended-height card and a 153.7 mm liquid card on a number nobody prints. And the C8's side radiator bay is specced two contradictory ways (a 100 mm thickness envelope and a separate 50 mm side-radiator depth clearance, no datum for either): if 50 mm is the real one, the 65 mm Astral rad does not fit, and with the floor reserved and the roof taking only one radiator, the second radiator would have nowhere to go. Nobody has asked Antec India in writing.

What would have to be true to switch: Antec confirms in writing that the side bay takes 65 mm, and confirms GPU height clearance ≥170 mm, and C4 comes back badly. All three — not any one. Two of those are questions nobody has ever asked Antec.

The Antec Performance 1 FT is dead on availability — out of stock at the only readable retailer, 230 mm wide, unpublished GPU height. Not a live option.
