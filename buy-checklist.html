Case decision · short version

Which case to buy

Four options, ranked. Everything else is on the build plan and the full comparison.

1

Lian Li O11 Dynamic EVO XL

Model O11DEXL-X, black · ₹23,200 (vishalperipherals) to ₹25,000 (MD Computers) · Buy this

Why it's best

  • 92.9 mm of open air under the graphics card. Your PRO 6000 breathes from underneath — this number is the whole decision. No other buyable case comes close (the Antec C8 has 62.8 mm, the Enthoo Pro 2 has 31.8 mm and it's sealed).
  • Everything fits with room to spare, verified against Lian Li's own CAD model, not marketing claims. The fat 65 mm radiator drops into the roof with 17 mm clear.
  • 304 mm wide with a separate PSU chamber — the roomiest of everything that qualified, and the PSU's heat never touches the air your GPU breathes.
  • You can actually buy it in India, from four shops. That's not a small thing — it's the reason the V3000 Plus is gone.

How to get full output from it

  1. Motherboard tray in the MIDDLE position. Not high, not low. Middle gives 82 mm roof / 90 mm floor. High kills your radiator, low starves your GPU. Tell the builder in writing.
  2. Astral 5090's radiator in the ROOF, blowing out. Route both EPS power cables before you screw the radiator in — you can't reach afterwards.
  3. CPU radiator on the SIDE, blowing in. Fresh room air. Fit the two supplied seal plates or the intake short-circuits.
  4. Three 140 mm fans on the floor, blowing in. Never a radiator there. Push them as far back and as far inboard as they go, or one of them blows past the card into empty space.
  5. One 120 mm fan at the rear, blowing out. There is no 140 mm rear mount.
  6. PRO 6000 in the lower slot, 5090 in the top slot. The lower card is the one that breathes from below. Never swap them.
  7. Install the fan daemon before the first real workload. This is worth more than the case choice: stock firmware sits at ~30% fan while the card hits 85–92 °C. Manual control is 15–19 °C, measured by multiple owners.

Pedestal: your carpenter builds it — spec below. Also still missing from your parts list: 2× NVMe drives. Buy those locally, not on Amazon (it shows import pricing).

⚠️ The one item to buy LAST, after the cards are seated.
There is only 30.5 mm between your two cards. A straight power cable needs 35 mm before it can bend — so an angled connector is mandatory, not optional. But CableMod's angled 12VHPWR adapters were recalled in the US in 2024 after 270+ melting reports. You want a cable with the 90° built into the connector, never an adapter stacked onto a straight one. Everything currently sold in India is the recalled type. Seat both cards, see which way each connector faces, then order.

Its real downsides

  • The glass can shatter. Not a rumour — a 314-point thread, one owner three times. Triggers are vibration, hard floors, over-tightened screws and frequent panel removal. Your rolled-pedestal plan hits three of four. Handle it by the steel, not the glass, and don't over-tighten the panel screws.
  • The floor filter is the only fresh-air path to your ₹7-lakh card. Fixable by design — see the pedestal spec below — but cleaning it becomes scheduled maintenance, not housekeeping.
  • Ships with zero fans (you only need 4, ~₹6,000) and the side intake is unfiltered.
  • After-sales is poor — Lian Li won't sell parts out of warranty, and Lian Li India is one phone number. You've said you don't care; noted, and it doesn't affect how the machine runs.
  • Possibly heading for EOL — persistently out of stock worldwide, no successor announced, and Lian Li didn't answer when an owner asked directly. That's a signal, not an announcement. Buying sooner costs nothing.
🪚 Pedestal spec — hand this to your carpenter
  • Open frame only. Rails under the four feet, nothing crossing the middle. A solid deck seals the floor intake and suffocates the GPU. This is the one rule that cannot be broken.
  • Clear opening at least 480 × 280 mm in the middle, so the whole floor filter area breathes.
  • Leave the filter's slide-out path completely clear — no rail, no lip, no caster in the way. This is the big win: you clean the filter without lifting a 40 kg machine.
  • Total height 25–50 mm including the castors. Whatever you choose, the finished stack has to sit below the new desk minimum you're programming — measure the assembled thing before setting that limit.
  • Rated for 60 kg+, four locking castors, steel or hardwood. Never foam or particle board.
  • Case footprint is 537 × 304 mm — that 537 includes the rear brackets that stick out past the published depth. Build to that, not to 522.
2

Phanteks NV9 MkII

SKU PH-NV923TG_DBK02 · ~₹18,499 if you can find it · Worth one phone call

Why it's #2 and not #1

Only because you probably can't buy it. On paper it beats the EVO XL at the one thing that still worries me. Amazon.in doesn't have it, EliteHubs only lists the smaller NV5, Vedant and TheITDepot have nothing. Computech and PC Studio might — their websites can't be read reliably from here.

📞 This is the only call worth making before you pay. Ring Computech and PC Studio and ask for PH-NV923TG_DBK02 by that code. If it's a no — buy the Lian Li and stop looking.

What it does better

  • 205 mm of GPU height clearance versus 169 mm. Your Astral 5090 would get 51 mm of space above it instead of 15.3 mm — that single number retires the tightest, most nervous clearance in your entire build.
  • 163 mm of roof space instead of 82 mm, and roof and floor are independent — no zero-sum tray decision to get wrong.
  • 140 mm rear fan, GPU support bracket included that actually fits a bottom-slot card, 280 mm wide, 99 L, no drive basement in the way.
  • Owners run this exact case with your exact CPU and a ROG 5090, with three floor fans as standard practice.

What's worse about it

  • Less air under the GPU — about 40 mm free versus the EVO XL's 65 mm. It wins on clearance and loses on the thing you bought a case for.
  • PSU bay is 210 mm against your 200 mm power supply; owners already call that bay the tight spot.
  • 615 mm tall, so it leans on the desk limit you're programming.
3

Antec C8

Plain black, non-ARGB · ₹9,599 (antecindia.in) · Only if budget forces it

Why it's #3

It's ₹14,000 cheaper and 56 mm shorter, and everything physically fits. But it gives your GPU 62.8 mm of air instead of 92.9 mm, and it publishes no GPU height clearance at all — so you'd be gambling your 137 mm and 153.7 mm cards on a number Antec doesn't print.

How you'd improve it

  • Ask Antec India in writing whether the side bay takes a 65 mm radiator. They publish two contradictory numbers (100 mm and 50 mm) and if 50 mm is the real one, your second radiator has nowhere to go at all.
  • Budget a short or 90° PSU cable kit — the bay is 210 mm excluding cables against your 200 mm PSU.
  • Buy the plain version, never the ARGB — its 160 mm bottom fans can't be moved or replaced.

Downsides

  • Only two shops in India stock it and three others have already sold out — the exact way you lost the V3000.
  • Its bare floor dust filter is fragile, and it's the only air path to your GPU.
  • Nothing about this build has been validated in it.
4

Phanteks Enthoo Pro 2

The one you found in stock · Don't buy it

Why not — in one paragraph

Its bottom fan mount sits inside the closed drive basement, under a solid steel lid. The only hole in that lid is a rubber cable grommet. So those bottom fans cool the power supply and the hard drives — they have no path to your graphics card at all. Your PRO 6000 would get about 31.8 mm of sealed dead air underneath it, against 92.9 mm of actively fed air in the Lian Li. Owners describe it themselves: "11 drives, 12 if you don't want bottom fans" — the fan positions compete with drive bays because they're in the same basement.

Second problem: it's 240 mm wide. That's 5 mm narrower than the Antec Flux Pro you already turned down for feeling cramped, and 64 mm narrower than the Lian Li. By volume it's the smaller case — 78 L against 84 L. Its "full tower" reputation comes from depth and height, not width.

About the vertical radiator thing you read: I haven't seen that thread, so I won't guess at what people were mocking — send me the link and I'll tell you exactly. But it doesn't change the answer. This case is out because of where its bottom fans sit, and no radiator arrangement fixes that.

To be fair to it: it's ~₹10,000 cheaper, genuinely well built, has a five-year warranty, a sealed filtered PSU chamber, and a steel-panel version with no glass at all — which is a real answer to the Lian Li's worst fault. It's an excellent case. It's just an excellent case for storage — every thread praising it is people fitting 20+ hard drives. It would be the right pick if your GPU were a blower card. It isn't.

✅ One thing that should settle your nerves
Puget Systems — who build RTX PRO 6000 workstations and sell them to NASA — put that exact card in a Fractal Define 7 XL: 240 mm wide with a solid, sound-damped front door. Narrower and more closed than what you're buying. And someone has already documented two RTX PRO 6000s running in a Lian Li O11 EVO XL. You're not doing anything exotic here.
